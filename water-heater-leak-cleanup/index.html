Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Pontiac

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Pontiac crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
